These are the moments where exact wording is the historical artifact:

1. **Putnam Patriot, 1894-07-20**, on the founding (Spirit p5):
   > "William E. Peck, Principal of St. Mark's School, Southborough, Mass., a brother of Reverend Mr. Peck of Putnam, has resigned his position to start a school of his own. ...    > "Mr. Peck did not hesitate to take the older boys into his confidence and after due consideration of other men, decided to turn again to St. Mark's and to invite its Senior Master, William Beach Olmsted, at the same time deciding to incorporate the school so that he might be relieved of its financial responsibility."

### What the book adds (proposed enrichments — NONE applied yet, awaiting your sign-off)

Three small additions could go into the description without bloating it:

1. **Family scale of the founding faculty.** Of the 6 founding teachers, four were Pecks: William (Latin), his brother Rev. Theodore M. Peck (English), his cousin Rev. Florus C. Peck (Episcopal pastor & teacher), and Harriet, who ran the infirmary. The current "the only females on campus at that time" line undersells Harriet's actual role.
2. **Geographic reach from day one.** Boarders came mostly from New York, with several from Ohio and Illinois — not just regional New England.
3. **Tuition.** $600 / year plus $30 incidentals; drawing and music extra.

### Proposed paraphrased rewrite (FOR YOUR REVIEW, not yet applied)

> *William E. Peck and his wife Harriet Benson Peck founded Pomfret School as an independent, Episcopal-affiliated, all-boys college preparatory boarding school. It opened on October 3, 1894 with 42 students and 6 teachers in the Main House of the Charles Grosvenor estate (formerly the Charles Grosvenor Inn). The founding faculty was largely a Peck family enterprise: William taught Latin; his brother Rev. Theodore M. Peck taught English; his cousin Rev. Florus C. Peck served as Episcopal pastor and teacher; and Harriet ran the school's infirmary. The Pecks' three daughters — Esther, Rachel, and Margaret — completed the family on campus. Tuition was $600 a year plus $30 for incidentals. Most boarders came from New York, with several traveling from as far as Ohio and Illinois.*

That's ~120 words vs. the current ~75. Trade-off: more specific and Pomfret-y; slightly longer card body. If too long, drop the tuition or the geography line.

### What the book documents that's missing

1. **Pomfret nearly closed.** The "struggle for survival" is not a euphemism — Deitch came in with a concrete brief: stop the bleeding or shut the doors. Enrollment had been declining since the early 1960s. The school carried a ~$500K operating deficit. 2. **The 1968 coeducation decision was followed by years of pure financial duress.** Useful framing context: female day students arrived in 1968 — but the school spent the next 8-10 years uncertain it would survive. The DEI history isn't a clean upward arc.
3. **First documented foreign exchange student** (Declan Randels, England, AFS program, ~1975-76).
